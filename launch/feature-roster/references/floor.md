# Floor

The few rules every video-skills film keeps, whatever its style. Everything about how a film looks or moves belongs to the
entry (`references/RECIPE.md`, `SWAP.md` "Never change"), and the entry wins on those. Only this file cannot be overridden.

A rule belongs here only if no good film of any style breaks it.

## Truth

- The product is real: its screens are captured from the product (signed out, demo data, or masked), never mocked up.
  Logos are the real files.
- Every claim about the product (names, numbers, features, prices, quotes) appears in the product's own site, docs or UI.
  Hooks, framing and transitions are free; claims are not.
- Anything invented to tell the story (a sample session, a worked example, illustrative data) is labelled on screen.
- Photorealistic generated footage is disclosed where the platform asks for it (YouTube: "altered or synthetic content").
- No other company's logos, characters or footage, including the reference's.

## Legibility

- No text cut off, overlapping, covered, or too small for the target screen (≥ 28 px at 1080 p for anything meant to be read).
- A 1-3 word label stays settled ≥ 0.8 s; a sentence ≈ 0.3 s per word, ≥ 1.2 s.
- Text sits on a ground it can be read on.

## Delivery

- Frame, safe zones and length follow the placement (16:9, 9:16 clear of platform UI, 4:5, 1:1).
- If the film has sound, master it to the platform's loudness (social and YouTube: about -14 LUFS, true peak under -1 dBTP).
- Frame 0 is a frame worth being the thumbnail.

## Process

1. Before building: the bar (format, platform, length, audience), the one-sentence idea, the beat map.
2. Before rendering: snapshot every scene and every transition, look at each frame, fix what fails above.
3. Before handing over: watch it at normal speed; check it muted (does it read?) and blind (does the sound hold?);
   name the three weakest moments and fix them; tell the user what you could not judge from stills.
4. Notes are taken by layer (story, picture, sound, text) as timestamp + keep + change. A note on one layer changes only that layer.
